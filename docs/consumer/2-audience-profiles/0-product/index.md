---
title: Product
description: Write product copy that helps customers see value and choose a next step.
---

## Reader

Write for a customer comparing options, deciding whether a product fits, or choosing what to do next. Product pages are **copy**: they make a clear case for an outcome. They are not neutral user guides or internal implementation notes.

## Goal

Lead with the customer's problem and the result the product enables. Match detail to the decision: comparisons need relevant differences and limits; a conversion page needs a specific next step. Explain a feature through its customer benefit, then support the claim with accurate evidence.

| Reader situation      | Lead with                  | Include                                          |
| --------------------- | -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Comparing options     | The need and product value | Relevant differences, limits, and specifications |
| Considering a feature | The outcome it enables     | How it works and evidence for the claim          |
| Ready to act          | The next useful step       | A clear action and what follows                  |

## Tone

Write with confidence and warmth. Use the customer's language and put the benefit before the mechanism. Be persuasive through relevant evidence, not hype, pressure, vague superlatives, or promises the product cannot support.

## Patterns

Use these patterns to help customers understand value and choose an informed next step.

### Testimonials

Use a testimonial to show a real customer's experience. Keep the customer's words intact, identify their role or context when approved, and connect the quote to a specific outcome. Add a short explanation that tells readers why this evidence matters; do not present praise as proof of an unverified product claim.

:::example[Testimonial template]

> “[Insert an approved customer quote, reproduced verbatim.]”
>
> — [Approved name or role]

This is a structure placeholder, not a customer statement. Replace it only with a genuine quote and approved attribution; verify the related product claim before publication.

:::

## Sources

Verify product behavior, specifications, comparisons, customer quotes, and outcomes with their current owner or approved evidence. Keep claim sources and permission records in the authoring workflow. Do not turn an inference into a customer promise.

## Validation

- Name the customer, their decision, and the outcome before drafting.
- Use direct, concrete language and a visible next step.
- Support factual and comparative claims with approved evidence.
- Keep technical detail only when it helps the customer's choice.
- Record assumptions and exclusions in the authoring brief; use `context` metadata only when the host supports it.
- Ask a reader unfamiliar with the product to explain the value, limits, and next step from the page alone.
